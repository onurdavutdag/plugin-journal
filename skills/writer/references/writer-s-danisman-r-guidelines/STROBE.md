# STROBE — Observational Studies (cohort / case-control / cross-sectional)

A 22-item checklist. The three designs (cohort, case-control, cross-sectional) share a common structure;
only items **6, 12, 14, 15** and the `*` footnote change by design — these are given below
with variant sub-notes. The item numbers are aligned with the official STROBE v4 checklist; the content
was verified one-to-one with the official STROBE checklist (cohort/case-control/cross-sectional 3 variants, von Elm et al. BMJ 2007).

## Title and Abstract
- **1.** (a) Indicate the study design with a common term in the title or abstract (e.g. "cohort",
  "case-control", "cross-sectional"). (b) Provide in the abstract an informative and
  balanced summary of what was done and what was found.

## Introduction
- **2. Background / rationale:** the scientific background and rationale for the reported research.
- **3. Objectives:** specific objectives; any pre-specified hypotheses.

## Methods
- **4. Study design:** present key elements of the design early in the paper.
- **5. Setting:** the setting, locations, and relevant dates — including the recruitment, exposure, follow-up,
  and data-collection periods.
- **6. Participants** — *design-specific*:
  - *Cohort:* (a) eligibility criteria, participant sources, and selection methods; describe follow-up
    methods. (b) In a matched study, matching criteria and the number of exposed/unexposed.
  - *Case-control:* (a) eligibility criteria, case ascertainment and control selection sources/methods;
    the rationale for case and control selection. (b) In a matched study, matching criteria and the
    **number of controls per case**.
  - *Cross-sectional:* (a) eligibility criteria, participant sources and selection methods. (**There is no 6b.**)
- **7. Variables:** clearly define all outcome, exposure, predictor, potential confounder, and effect
  modifier variables; give diagnostic criteria if any.
- **8.\* Data sources / measurement:** for each variable, detail of the data source and the assessment (measurement)
  method. If more than one group, the comparability of assessment methods.
- **9. Bias:** the measures taken to address potential sources of bias.
- **10. Study size:** how the sample size was arrived at.
- **11. Quantitative variables:** how quantitative variables were handled in the analysis; if grouping was done,
  which groupings and why.
- **12. Statistical methods** — (a) all methods including confounder control; (b) subgroup and
  interaction examination; (c) how missing data were handled; (e) sensitivity analyses. **(d)
  *design-specific*:**
  - *Cohort:* how loss to follow-up was handled.
  - *Case-control:* how the case-control matching was handled in the analysis.
  - *Cross-sectional:* analysis methods that account for the sampling strategy.

## Results
- **13.\* Participants:** (a) the numbers at each stage (potentially eligible, examined for eligibility,
  confirmed eligible, included, completing follow-up, analyzed); (b) reasons for non-participation at each stage;
  (c) **consider using a flow diagram**.
- **14.\* Descriptive data:** (a) participant characteristics (demographic, clinical, social) and
  exposure/potential confounder information; (b) the number of participants with missing data for each variable.
  **(c) *cohort only*:** summarize the follow-up time (e.g. average and total).
- **15.\* Outcome data** — *design-specific*:
  - *Cohort:* event numbers over time or summary measures.
  - *Case-control:* the numbers in each exposure category or summary measures of exposure.
  - *Cross-sectional:* outcome-event numbers or summary measures.
- **16. Main results:** (a) unadjusted and, if any, confounder-adjusted estimates + precision
  (**95% CI**); which confounders were adjusted for and why. (b) If a continuous variable was categorized,
  the category boundaries. (c) If relevant, consider translating the relative risk into absolute risk for a
  meaningful time period.
- **17. Other analyses:** subgroup, interaction, and sensitivity analyses.

## Discussion
- **18. Key results:** a summary of the key findings with reference to the objectives.
- **19. Limitations:** discuss the limitations, considering potential sources of bias/imprecision; discuss the
  **direction and magnitude** of the bias. **Mandatory paragraph.**
- **20. Interpretation:** a cautious overall interpretation considering objectives, limitations, analysis
  multiplicity, similar studies, and other evidence.
- **21. Generalizability:** the external validity of the results.

## Other
- **22. Funding:** the funding source and the funder's role; if relevant, its role in the original study on which
  the article is based.

## Footnote `*` (design-specific)
- *Cohort / cross-sectional:* for items 8, 13, 14, 15 give the information **separately for the exposed and
  unexposed groups**.
- *Case-control:* for the same items give the information **separately for cases and controls**.

## Commonly skipped (writer checklist)
- **Flow diagram** (13c) and **missing data** (12c, 14b) — missing in most theses.
- The **confounder** list and why they were selected (16a) must be written explicitly.
- The **direction and magnitude** of the bias (19) must be discussed in the limitations paragraph.
- Select the design-specific item correctly: in case-control 12d = matching, in cross-sectional 12d = sampling
  strategy; 14c only in cohort (follow-up time).
