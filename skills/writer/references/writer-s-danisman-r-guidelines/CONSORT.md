# CONSORT 2010 — Randomized Controlled Trials (RCT)

A 25-item checklist (CONSORT 2010, BMJ 2010;340:c332). It is based on an individually randomized, two-group,
parallel-group RCT (cluster/non-inferiority designs need an additional extension). The item
numbers are aligned with the official CONSORT 2010; the content was derived one-to-one from the official checklist.

## Title and Abstract
- **1a.** Identification as a randomized trial in the title.
- **1b.** A structured summary of the trial design, methods, results, and conclusions (for specific guidance,
  CONSORT for Abstracts).

## Introduction
- **2a. Background and rationale:** the scientific background and explanation of the rationale.
- **2b. Objectives:** specific objectives or hypotheses.

## Methods
- **3a. Trial design:** a description of the design (parallel, factorial, etc.), including the allocation
  ratio.
- **3b.** Important changes to the methods after the trial started (e.g. eligibility criteria)
  and their reasons.
- **4a. Participants:** participant eligibility criteria.
- **4b.** The settings and locations where the data were collected.
- **5. Interventions:** the interventions for each group with enough detail to allow replication; including
  **how and when** they were actually administered.
- **6a. Outcomes:** completely defined, pre-specified primary and secondary outcome
  measures; including how and when they were assessed.
- **6b.** Any changes to outcomes after the trial started and their reasons.
- **7a. Sample size:** how it was determined.
- **7b.** When applicable, an explanation of any interim analyses and stopping guidelines.
- **Randomization:**
  - **8a. Sequence generation:** the method used to generate the random allocation sequence.
  - **8b.** The type of randomization; details of any restriction (such as blocking and block size).
  - **9. Allocation concealment mechanism:** the mechanism used to implement the random allocation sequence (e.g.
    sequentially numbered closed containers); a description of the steps taken to conceal the sequence until
    interventions were assigned.
  - **10. Implementation:** who generated the sequence, who enrolled participants, who assigned participants to
    interventions.
- **11a. Blinding:** if done, who was blinded after assignment (e.g. participants, care providers,
  outcome assessors) and how.
- **11b.** If relevant, a description of the similarity of the interventions.
- **12a. Statistical methods:** the methods used to compare groups for the primary and secondary outcomes.
- **12b.** Methods for additional analyses (such as subgroup and adjusted analyses).

## Results (a flow diagram is strongly recommended)
- **13a. Participant flow:** for each group, the numbers randomly assigned, receiving the intended treatment, and
  analyzed for the primary outcome.
- **13b.** For each group, losses and exclusions after randomization, together with reasons.
- **14a. Recruitment:** the dates defining the periods of recruitment and follow-up.
- **14b.** Why the trial ended or was stopped.
- **15. Baseline data:** a **table** showing baseline demographic and clinical characteristics for each group.
- **16. Numbers analyzed:** for each group, the number of participants included in each analysis (denominator) and
  whether the analysis was **by the original assigned groups**.
- **17a. Outcomes and estimation:** for each primary and secondary outcome, the results for each group, the
  estimated **effect size and its precision (e.g. 95% CI)**.
- **17b.** For binary outcomes, presentation of both absolute and relative effect sizes is recommended.
- **18. Ancillary analyses:** the results of any other analyses performed (including subgroup and adjusted
  analyses); distinguishing pre-specified from exploratory.
- **19. Harms:** all important harms or unintended effects in each group (for specific guidance,
  CONSORT for Harms).

## Discussion
- **20. Limitations:** the trial's limitations; sources of potential bias, imprecision, and, if relevant,
  analysis multiplicity.
- **21. Generalizability:** the external validity/applicability of the findings.
- **22. Interpretation:** an interpretation consistent with the results; the balancing of benefits and harms;
  consideration of other relevant evidence.

## Other Information
- **23. Registration:** the trial registration number and the name of the registry.
- **24. Protocol:** where the full trial protocol can be accessed, if available.
- **25. Funding:** the sources of funding and other support (e.g. supply of drugs), the role of the funders.

## Commonly skipped (writer checklist)
- **Flow diagram** (13a) and **allocation concealment** (9) — the most commonly missing in RCTs.
- The **registration number** (23) and that the analysis was **by the original assigned groups** (16, an explicit
  statement instead of ITT) must be stated.
- For the primary outcome, the **effect size + 95% CI** (17a) — the p-value alone is insufficient.
- Baseline characteristics as a **table** (15); **how** the blinding was done (11a).
