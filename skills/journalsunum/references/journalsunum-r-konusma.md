# Talk types — congress, thesis defence, seminar / journal club

The four oral formats differ by **parameters**, not by method: the same narrative arc
(`journalsunum-r-yapi.md`) is budgeted differently. This file holds the parameters, the
timing rules, and the Q&A / backup-slide expectations per type. The poster is a different
artefact and lives in `journalsunum-r-poster.md`.

## 1. The one rule under everything: one slide ≈ one minute

Plan for ~1 slide per minute, then adjust: a data-heavy result slide takes 2–3 minutes, a
section divider 15–30 seconds, a key finding 2–4 minutes, and every build step counts as its
own slide. Spend **40–50 % of the time on Results**. Never let the introduction exceed
15–20 % of the talk.

| Duration | Total slides | Title/Intro | Methods | Results | Discussion | Conclusion |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 5 min (lightning / poster spotlight) | 5–7 | 1–2 | 0–1 | 2–3 | 1 | 1 |
| 8–10 min (congress, short) | 8–12 | 2 | 1–2 | 4–5 | 2–3 | 1 |
| 15 min (congress, standard) | 15–18 | 2–3 | 2–3 | 6–8 | 3–4 | 1–2 |
| 20 min (congress, extended) | 20–24 | 3 | 3–4 | 8–10 | 4–5 | 2 |
| 30 min (seminar / journal club) | 25–30 | 3–4 | 5–6 | 10–12 | 6–8 | 2 |
| 45 min (defence / invited seminar) | 35–45 | 4–5 | 8–10 | 15–20 | 8–10 | 2–3 |
| 60 min (defence, long / lecture) | 45–60 | 5–6 | 10–12 | 20–25 | 10–12 | 3–4 |

If Q&A is **inside** the slot, cut content slides by 20–30 % and finish 2–3 minutes early.
If Q&A is **after** the slot, use the full time and finish exactly on it.

## 2. Congress oral paper (kongre sözlü bildiri)

**Context:** 8–20 minutes (10 or 15 most common in Turkish congresses), concurrent
sessions, a mixed specialist/non-specialist audience that has already sat through many
talks, strict timing, often recorded, 2–5 minutes of questions after.

**What it must do:** communicate 1–2 key findings, make the audience want the paper or the
poster, and stop on time. Not: reproduce the manuscript.

**15-minute allocation** (scale proportionally for 10 or 20):

```
Introduction  2–3 min, 2–3 slides   title + hook 30 s · background 90 s · question 60 s
Methods       2–3 min, 2–3 slides   design · key procedure · analysis
Results       6–7 min, 6–8 slides   primary finding 2–3 min · secondary 2 min · validation 2 min
Discussion    2–3 min, 3–4 slides   interpretation · prior work · implications · limitations
Conclusion    1 min,   1–2 slides   take-home · acknowledgements
Buffer        1–2 min
```

**Checkpoints while speaking (15 min):** 3–4 min → introduction over; 7–8 min → halfway
through results; 12–13 min → starting conclusions.

**Do / don't:**
- Open with a hook (a surprising number, a compelling image); preview the main finding early.
  Do not open with "I'm honoured to be here", do not spend more than 2 minutes on background.
- Focus on 1–2 findings; show data, not only conclusions. Do not include every analysis
  from the paper, do not go into method detail the question does not need.
- Practise to exact time. Running over is the single most unprofessional thing a speaker
  can do; rushing the end is the second.

**Q&A:** prepare 2–4 backup slides with the extra data a sceptic will ask for
(subgroups, sensitivity analyses, the full Table 1). Keep answers 30–60 seconds; send detail
seekers to the poster or the paper.

**Lightning (5–7 min) and poster spotlight (3 min):** one message only, no methods beyond
one sentence, maximise visuals; the spotlight ends with the poster number and a QR.

## 3. Thesis defence (tez savunması)

**Context:** 30–60 minutes of presentation, then 30–90 minutes of examination. The jury has
read the thesis; the audience may include colleagues and family. Formal. The talk is judged
on mastery of the field and on the candidate's independence, not on novelty alone.

**45-minute allocation:**

```
Introduction       5 min,   5–6 slides    context · central question · overview of studies · roadmap
Literature review  5 min,   4–5 slides    framework · key prior findings · gaps · your contribution
Study 1            8–10 min, 10–12 slides question · methods · results · interim conclusion
Study 2            8–10 min, 10–12 slides (same)
Study 3 (optional) 8–10 min, 10–12 slides (same)
General discussion 8–10 min, 8–10 slides  synthesis · theoretical + practical implications ·
                                          limitations (comprehensive) · future directions
Conclusions        2–3 min, 2–3 slides    contributions · closing · acknowledgements
```

A single-study thesis collapses Study 1–3 into one longer block with a fuller Methods
section (design rationale, sample size justification, analysis plan, validation).

**Do / don't:**
- Cover every study; connect them explicitly ("Study 1 left X open, Study 2 asked it").
- Address limitations **before** the jury does, and do not minimise them; do not oversell,
  do not hide null results.
- Include an outline slide and return to it as section dividers — the jury tracks progress.

**Q&A approach:** listen to the whole question, pause, answer, refer to a slide or a thesis
section by number. Prepare a backup set of 8–15 slides: full tables, alternative analyses,
excluded-case reasoning, power calculation, every figure the jury might want larger.
Difficult-question templates: *method critique* → acknowledge, explain rationale, note in
future work; *alternative interpretation* → "worth exploring; I focused on X because…";
*why not Y?* → "valuable; given [constraint] I prioritised…"; *inconsistency* → "one
explanation is…".

## 4. Seminar / journal club / case presentation (seminer, olgu, literatür)

**Context:** 20–45 minutes, department or clinic audience with domain knowledge,
interruptions welcome, heavy discussion afterwards. Two sub-types share the parameters:
- **Own-work seminar** — a deeper version of the congress talk: more methods, more than one
  finding, failed attempts and pivots are welcome, unpublished work is fine.
- **Journal club / literature review** — presenting *someone else's* paper or a topic:
  critical analysis is the point; the audience may have read the paper.

**30-minute journal-club allocation:**

```
Context            2–3 min, 2–3 slides   citation · why this paper · significance
Introduction       3–4 min, 2–3 slides   question · prior work · hypotheses
Methods            5–7 min, 4–6 slides   design · participants · procedures · analysis · your assessment
Results            8–10 min, 5–7 slides  main findings · key figures explained · your interpretation
Discussion         5–7 min, 3–4 slides   authors' interpretation · strengths · limitations · implications
Critical analysis  3–5 min, 1–2 slides   what we learned · open questions · relevance to us
```

**Do / don't:** explain the paper (not everyone read it) but never merely summarise; do not
skip methods — they are usually where the critique lives; be fair but critical; pose open
questions to the room ("is this the right control?"). For an own-work seminar, welcome
interruptions and use checkpoint questions ("does this make sense so far?").

**Case presentation (olgu sunumu):** presentation → differential → workup → management →
outcome → teaching points, 10–20 minutes; the teaching points are the conclusion slide.

## 5. Timing per slide type

| Slide | Time |
|---|---|
| Title / section divider | 15–30 s |
| 3–4 bullets | ~1 min; 5–6 bullets ~2 min (15–20 s per bullet, never read verbatim) |
| Data figure | 2–3 min: set-up 30 s · walk the pattern 60 s · main finding 30 s · statistics 30 s · transition 30 s |
| Multi-panel figure | 2–4 min; build it panel by panel or split into slides |
| Table | 1–2 min; guide attention ("look at the top row"), never read cells |

## 6. Running over or under

**Behind:** skip the backup / secondary slides you marked in advance, summarise rather than
detail, cut Discussion before Results, **never skip the conclusion**, never say "I'll go
quickly through these" — just skip. **Ahead:** expand a key point, add an example, take a
question; do not add filler or slow down artificially.

**Plan B is prepared, not improvised:** know which slides are must-keep, mark optional ones
in the notes, and keep a 5-minute version of every 15-minute talk in your head.

## 7. Practice minimums

| Type | Runs | Time |
|---|---|---|
| Lightning / spotlight | 5–7 (10+ for 3-minute) | 3 h |
| Congress 15 min | 3–5 | 4–5 h |
| Seminar 45 min | 3–4 | 6–8 h |
| Defence 60 min | 4–6 | 10–15 h |

Run 1 rough (it will run long) · run 2 transitions · run 3 exact timing with a visible timer
· run 4 recorded, watch for fillers · run 5 opening and closing only, the day before.

returns: the slide budget per section (from §1 and the type's allocation), the checkpoints,
